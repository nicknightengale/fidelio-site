Households and Film Societies

A household shares one Library, wishlist and set of programmes, and one Fidelio House Membership. Anyone in the household can add to and change what’s shared.

A Film Society is a group of members who share their shelves and talk about films. The member who looks after a society can remove someone; a removed member can rejoin only with a new invitation. When the member who looks after a society leaves, the member who joined earliest takes over.

Lending discs. Members of a Film Society can lend each other discs, and a member can keep a record of a disc lent to someone outside Fidelio. A loan is between the two people. Fidelio keeps the record only: it never holds, insures or guarantees a disc, and isn’t responsible if one is lost, damaged or returned late. Loans are free: don’t charge for one or ask for a deposit. Anything that goes wrong with a loan is for the two of you to settle. Blocking a member closes your conversation with them but doesn’t end a loan; deleting your account ends your loans.

Showings. Members of a Film Society can post a showing: a film on a disc, at a time and place, with a number of seats. Members of the society, and guests the host invites by link, can take a seat. A showing is a private get-together among the host’s society and guests: it’s free, so don’t charge for a seat or for entry. The host, and any co-hosts they add, are responsible for their place and for who they invite, and everyone who goes is responsible for themselves. Add photos to a showing’s album only of people who are happy to be in them; we take a photo down when someone in it asks. Fidelio lists the showing and its seats only: it doesn’t organise, check or supervise a showing, and isn’t responsible for what happens there. Report and Block work on showings as they do on messages.

Messages

Members can write to each other in Messages: one to one, in their household’s thread, and in their Film Societies’ chats. Every conversation and chat outside your household has Report and Block, and members can report a member’s profile: their photo, their name or something on their shelf. Messages containing words on our blocked list aren’t sent, and there’s a limit on how many messages one member can send in a minute and in a day.

A person at Fidelio reviews every report within 24 hours, every day. We don’t read your conversations. We look at one only when it’s reported, and then only the copy kept with the report, or when a security problem or the law requires it. Messages aren’t end-to-end encrypted; they’re stored securely with the rest of your Fidelio data. If someone you write to has Shared inbox on, their household-mate can read your conversation too.

You choose who sees each post: everyone in Fidelio, your Film Societies or your household. Replies are seen by whoever can see the post. A post we take down is removed everywhere, with its replies.
