Privacy

What Fidelio keeps, and why

Effective November 3, 2026. Posted October 27, 2026; until November 3, the version of October 27, 2026 applies. What changed.

This policy covers the Fidelio app for iPhone (on the App Store as Fidelio Cinema) and fidelio.film, including its waitlist. Fidelio is published by Fidelio Cinema LLC.

What we collect

Your account. You sign in to the app with Apple. Apple gives us an email address: your own, or a private address that forwards to you if you choose Hide My Email. We don’t ask Apple for your name, and we never see your Apple password.

What you tell us about yourself. If you choose, a first name and a region, in your own words. Both are optional, and you can change or clear them in Account. Your region is only what you type; your phone’s location is never sent.

What you keep in Fidelio. Your Library (the editions you own, their Shelf Numbers, and the price you paid and the date you bought them, when you enter them), your wishlist, the programmes you make, your Lobby Cards and the answers on your profile, the labels you follow, your stores and your settings, including who sees your shelf.

Messages. The words and photos you send other members, who sent them and when, and when you last read each conversation. Before a photo leaves your phone, its location and camera details are removed.

Film Societies. The societies you start or join, when you joined, and each society’s history lines (who joined, left or renamed it), which carry the first name you had at the time.

Second Run. The copies you list: their photos, note, condition, price, shipping cost, whether you ship or offer pickup, and when each was listed, updated and sold.

Reports and blocks. If you report a conversation or a listing, we keep the reason, your note if you add one, and a copy of what was reported: for a conversation, its last 50 messages with their photos, marked only by which side wrote each. If you block someone, we keep that you did, and when.

Your agreements. When you agreed to our Terms, and which version.

The season ticket. If your household buys a season ticket, Apple tells us which kind it is, when it starts, renews or ends, and whether it was refunded. We read this directly from Apple, with no other billing company in between, and keep it with your household’s records, including our backups. We never see your payment details.

Notifications. If you turn on notifications, Apple gives us a token for your iPhone so we can send them, and we keep the time you chose for your daily round-up and your time zone.

Your invitation. Fidelio is by invitation. When you enter an invite code, we record that it admitted you. We keep only a fingerprint of each code, never the code itself. Film Society invitation links are kept the same way.

The waitlist. On fidelio.film, the waitlist keeps the email address you enter, the labels and devices you choose, and when you joined.

Technical details. For each device you’re signed in on, we keep its IP address and device type while you stay signed in on it, and for no more than 90 days after you last use the app there. Signing out removes it at once, and deleting your account removes all of them. The company that hosts our database also logs IP addresses, for 7 days, and GitHub, which hosts this website, logs visits under its own policy. We use these only to run and protect Fidelio.

What stays on your phone

Location. Around Town can use your location to show stores in your city. It is worked out on your phone, at city level, and is never stored or sent to us.

Searches. What you search for is sent only to find editions, and isn’t kept with your account.

The barcode scanner. It reads barcodes on your phone. Only the barcode’s number is sent, to find the edition, and the scanner’s pictures are never saved or sent. Photos are sent only when you choose to send one in a message or add one to a listing.

What we don’t do

Fidelio carries no advertising. We don’t sell or rent your information, share it for advertising, or track you across other companies’ apps and websites. The app has no advertising or analytics trackers. The website sets no cookies and uses no outside scripts or fonts, and no other company tracks visitors across websites through it. It behaves the same whether or not your browser sends a Do Not Track signal, because there is no such tracking to turn off.

How we use it

To run Fidelio for you: keeping your Library and wishlist, showing prices for the editions you want, delivering your messages, running your Film Societies and Second Run listings, sending the notifications and emails you choose, reviewing reports, and answering you when you write. On the waitlist, to send your invitation and news of the opening, and to decide which labels to cover first.

The Digest. Members get the Digest, an email about the editions, labels and reviews they follow. It comes weekly unless you choose daily or off, in the app or from the links in any Digest; those links work without signing in, and one of them stops all our marketing email. Each Digest names Fidelio Cinema LLC and carries our mailing address. It goes to your account’s email, including a Hide My Email address if you chose one.

Who sees it

Your household. Members of your household share its Library, wishlist and programmes, and see each other’s Lobby Cards. Copies you add carry your first name, if you’ve given one. If your household turns on Shared inbox, your household-mates can read your conversations too, including messages other members send you.

Your shelf. Who sees your shelf beyond your household is your choice in Shelf settings: everyone on Fidelio, your Film Societies, or only your household. Shelf Numbers, the prices you paid and the dates you bought are never shown outside your household.

Film Societies. People in a society see its members’ first names, its history lines, and the shelves of members who chose to show theirs to it.

Members you write to. They see your first name and what you send, and so do their household-mates if their household turns on Shared inbox.

Second Run. When you list a copy, members who view the listing see your first name, your region, when you joined Fidelio, the Lobby Card you choose for your listings (even if it’s hidden on your shelf), your shelf as your Shelf settings allow, and the listing itself. In conversations about a listing, they also see the price it was first listed at. A household-mate’s name is never shown to buyers.

Lobby Cards. Your household sees them. A card you share yourself goes wherever you send it, and the card you choose for your listings is seen by members viewing them.

Reports. A report goes to Fidelio, never to the member reported, and nobody is told who reported. A person at Fidelio reviews each one within 24 hours, every day. Each report is emailed to support@fidelio.film with only its kind, reason, time and a reference number: no message text, notes, names or email addresses.

Service providers. Each handles your information only to provide its service to Fidelio, and is bound to protect it at least as well as this policy does:

  • Supabase hosts Fidelio’s database, sign-in, and the photos in messages and listings.
  • DigitalOcean runs Fidelio’s servers (from November 2026).
  • Backblaze holds encrypted backup copies (from November 2026).
  • Resend sends Fidelio’s email.
  • GitHub hosts fidelio.film.
  • Anthropic provides the Claude AI tools that help us run and check Fidelio’s systems, and may process member information when we do.
  • Apple runs Sign in with Apple, the App Store, season ticket purchases and notifications, and hosts our support mailbox (iCloud Mail). If you test Fidelio through TestFlight, Apple passes us the feedback and crash reports you choose to send.

Stores. Some store links are affiliate links. If you follow one, the store and its affiliate network may record that the visit came from Fidelio, under their own policies. What you do at the store is between you and the store. The store or its network may tell us that a purchase was made and what commission we earned, but never your name or payment details. More on how Fidelio is paid is on the About page.

The law. We share information if the law requires it, or to protect Fidelio’s members or Fidelio Cinema LLC.

If Fidelio changes hands. If Fidelio Cinema LLC is sold, merges with another company, or transfers Fidelio to another company, your information goes with Fidelio to the new owner, who must keep to this policy. Any change to it after that follows the Changes section below. We tell members in the app before such a transfer happens. A company considering buying Fidelio may see member information only under a confidentiality agreement, and only as far as it needs to.

How long we keep it

We keep your information while you have an account, with these exceptions.

Messages are kept while either member in the conversation still has an account. If you delete a message, its words and photo are removed from the conversation at once, leaving a line that says a message was deleted.

Reports are kept until they’re answered, then for 1 year, and deleted with any photos only they still held. Report emails are deleted from our mailbox after 1 year.

Film Societies. If you leave a society, your first name stays in its history lines while the society lasts. Invitation links are deleted 30 days after they expire. A society ends, with its history, when its last member leaves.

Backups. We back up members’ information every night and delete each backup after 28 days. Copies stored off our own equipment are encrypted before they leave it.

Deleting your account. In the app, open Account and choose Delete account. When you delete your account, we delete it from our systems straight away, and any copies in our backups are deleted within 30 days. Your sign-in, name, region, followed labels, settings, stores, blocks and your record of agreeing to the Terms go with it (except a record the law requires us to keep, for as long as it does), and your email is removed from the waitlist. Messages you sent stay in the other member’s conversation, signed “Former member”; your photos in them are deleted, except a photo a kept report still holds, which goes when that report’s year is up. Your first name in Film Society history lines becomes “Former member”, and in a society you looked after, the member who joined earliest takes over. If you share a household, the copies and wishes you added stay in its Library without your name. If you were the only member of your household, we keep its Library for 30 days in case you change your mind, then delete it; any backup copies of it are deleted within 7 days after that. To keep it, write to support@fidelio.film within those 30 days, from the same email address or by replying to any email we’ve sent you. Your account itself can’t be brought back.

To stop Sign in with Apple for Fidelio as well, open your iPhone’s Settings, tap your name, then Sign-In & Security, and remove Fidelio.

Your choices

To see, correct or delete the information we hold, or to leave the waitlist, write to support@fidelio.film. We answer within 30 days. You can turn notifications off in the app or in your iPhone’s Settings, change or stop the Digest from its links, and reply to any email from Fidelio to stop receiving more.

Fidelio is for people 13 and over, and Second Run for people 18 and over. Fidelio is not meant for children under 13, and we don’t knowingly collect their information.

Changes

We post every new version of this policy on this page with its date and a short summary of what changed. We tell members in the app at least 7 days before a change takes effect. If a change affects what we collect, what we share or with whom, or who sees it, the app asks you to agree again on the day it takes effect.
